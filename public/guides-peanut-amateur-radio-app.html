Peanut Amateur Radio App: Beginner Setup and Operating Guide

A beginner guide to the Peanut amateur radio PTT app, including access codes, rooms, D-STAR/Fusion/DMR links, audio setup, first contacts and safe operating.

← All guides

What Peanut is

Peanut is an internet PTT application for amateur radio operators. The client connects to the Peanut server, which acts as a reflector where operators can meet. Some Peanut rooms are connected to D-STAR, System Fusion and DMR reflectors.

What you need

You need a valid amateur-radio callsign and a Peanut access code. Use the developer's current instructions because registration and room availability can change.

Official information: PA7LIM Peanut information ↗

Install and prepare

  1. Use the official Peanut information page to find the current supported downloads.
  2. Install the app from the recommended source.
  3. Follow the developer's process to obtain the required code.
  4. Enter your own callsign and code.
  5. Allow microphone access and select suitable audio.

Rooms and linked systems

Choose a room and listen before transmitting. A room may be internet-only or may connect onward to an amateur digital reflector. Do not assume every room reaches RF. Check the current room list and information from the operator.

Making a first call

Listen first, press PTT, pause briefly, then identify with your callsign and say you are listening. Keep the first transmission short while you confirm audio and routing.

Audio tips

A headset can reduce feedback. Keep microphone gain moderate, avoid holding the phone too close to your mouth, and test on an appropriate room rather than repeatedly keying a busy linked reflector.

Peanut versus EchoLink and AllStar

They are separate systems. EchoLink has its own validated directory and RF link/repeater nodes; AllStarLink is based around linked Asterisk nodes; Peanut uses its own server and rooms, with some rooms bridged to digital reflectors. Your credentials for one system do not automatically grant access to another.

Operating etiquette

Use your callsign, leave gaps between overs, avoid monopolising linked rooms, respect room rules and remember that your audio may be heard on systems beyond the app itself.
