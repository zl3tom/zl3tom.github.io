Amateur Radio From Your Phone: Apps, EchoLink, AllStar, APRS and More

Use a phone for amateur radio with EchoLink, QSO One, RepeaterPhone, Peanut, APRS, QRZ logging, Zello/network radios, remote stations and other useful tools.

← All guides

Your phone can be a serious radio companion

A phone can help you communicate through internet-linked amateur systems, find repeaters, track APRS, log contacts, look up callsigns, control remote equipment and monitor propagation. Some activities are radio support tools; others can carry your voice into an amateur-radio network.

EchoLink

EchoLink lets validated amateur operators connect to other users, repeaters and link nodes. Official mobile options are available, and your phone can be an EchoLink node in its own right.

After validation, you can search for ZL3TOM-L, EchoLink node 304602, as a practical node to try. My link is associated with my multimode system and may also be connected into ANZEL Radio ↗.

QSO One

QSO One brings several linked voice systems into one interface and can be useful for operators who want access to systems such as EchoLink, AllStarLink, DMR, System Fusion and M17 from a supported device. Follow its current official platform and account requirements.

RepeaterPhone on iPhone and iPad

RepeaterPhone ↗ is designed for licensed amateur operators on Apple devices. It supports connections to EchoLink and AllStarLink nodes, directory searching, favourites, Bluetooth audio and Apple Watch PTT controls. You need the appropriate EchoLink or AllStarLink account.

Peanut

Peanut is a PTT client for licensed amateurs that connects to the Peanut server and rooms; some rooms are linked to D-STAR, Fusion and DMR reflectors. It requires a Peanut access code. See the dedicated Peanut guide on this site for setup and operating information.

APRS

APRS apps and web tools can show station positions, messages and other packet information. APRS.fi is useful for viewing stations, while platform-specific apps may provide transmit or messaging features. Think about privacy before beaconing a precise location.

Logging and QRZ

Your phone browser can access QRZ and many web logbooks, making it easy to look up callsigns and enter contacts while portable. Record UTC time, callsign, band and mode accurately. For offline or portable operation, choose a logger that can export standard ADIF files.

Zello and network radio

Zello and similar PTT services can turn a normal smartphone into a network-radio client. These internet conversations are different from direct RF amateur transmissions, although some communities operate authorised links into amateur systems.

Remote stations

Remote-control systems can let a licensed operator control a real amateur transceiver over the internet. Because the transmitter is real RF equipment, you must follow the licence rules applying to the station and transmitter location, as well as the remote service's rules.

Other useful phone tools

Consider repeater directories, propagation and solar-weather tools, satellite pass predictors, Maidenhead grid locators, antenna calculators, digital-mode references, band-plan references, Morse practice, SDR receivers and club/net calendars. Install from trusted developer or app-store sources and check permissions before giving an app microphone, location or account access.
