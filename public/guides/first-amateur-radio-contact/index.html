ZL3TOM beginner guide

How to Make Your First Amateur Radio Contact

Exactly what to say on your first amateur radio contact, including calling CQ, answering a CQ, callsigns, signal reports, repeaters, digital voice and ending a QSO.

← All guides

Before you transmit

Check that you are authorised to transmit, choose a permitted frequency and mode, set sensible power, then listen before pressing PTT. On a repeater or linked system, listen long enough to understand whether a conversation or net is already in progress.

Your callsign is your on-air identity

Say your callsign clearly. Do not rush it. If the other operator misses part of it, use the international phonetic alphabet. For example: Zulu Lima Three Tango Oscar Mike.

Calling CQ on HF

EXAMPLE
CQ CQ CQ, this is ZL3TOM, Zulu Lima Three Tango Oscar Mike, calling CQ and listening.

Pause and listen. If nobody answers, call again. Avoid endlessly repeating CQ without giving listeners time to reply.

Answering a CQ

EXAMPLE
[Their callsign], this is ZL3TOM, Zulu Lima Three Tango Oscar Mike.

Once acknowledged, exchange names, locations and a signal report. Keep your first contact simple; you do not need to know every piece of radio jargon.

Signal reports

For voice contacts, R-S reports describe readability and signal strength. A report such as 5 by 9 means perfectly readable with a very strong signal. Give the report you actually observe rather than automatically saying 59.

Repeaters and FM

You normally do not call CQ repeatedly on a local repeater. Listen first, then identify and say you are listening.

REPEATER EXAMPLE
ZL3TOM listening.

If joining an existing conversation, wait for the pause between stations and identify. Leave a short gap between overs so another station can break in.

Digital voice and linked systems

On DMR, EchoLink, AllStar, System Fusion, M17 and other linked systems, key up and pause briefly before speaking so the network has time to establish the audio path. Large talkgroups may reach many repeaters and users, so keep test transmissions short.

DMR EXAMPLE
ZL3TOM listening on Talkgroup 91.

What to talk about

A first QSO can be as simple as callsign, name, location, signal report and the equipment you are using. Ask the other operator about their station or local conditions. Normal friendly conversation is fine.

Ending the QSO

EXAMPLE
Thanks for the contact. 73 from ZL3TOM. Clear and listening.

On HF you can simply thank the station, exchange 73 and identify. On a linked node, disconnect only if you made the connection and local practice requires it.

Afterwards

Write down the other callsign, UTC date and time, band, mode and any useful notes. You can then enter the contact into your preferred logbook and, where appropriate, request or confirm a QSL.
