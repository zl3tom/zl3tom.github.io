Amateur Radio Hotspots Explained: Pi-Star, WPSD, DMR and Digital Voice

A beginner guide to amateur radio hotspots, Pi-Star, WPSD, DMR, D-STAR, YSF and M17, including how hotspots work and how they differ from repeaters.

← All guides

What is an amateur radio hotspot?

A digital-voice hotspot is a small personal gateway between a compatible radio and an internet-connected digital voice network. Your handheld normally makes a short-range RF connection to the hotspot; the hotspot then carries the digital voice traffic over the internet to the selected network, reflector, room or talkgroup.

What equipment is inside?

Many hotspots combine a small computer, such as a Raspberry Pi or similar board, with an MMDVM-compatible radio modem. Some are complete commercial devices. The modem handles the local digital RF side while software manages networking, modes and configuration.

Pi-Star and WPSD

Pi-Star became a widely used hotspot and repeater software platform for digital voice. WPSD is another dashboard/software distribution used with compatible hotspots and digital voice systems. These are software platforms rather than radio networks themselves: the configured hotspot can connect onward to supported networks and protocols.

DMR hotspots

With DMR, a hotspot can connect your DMR handheld to services such as BrandMeister or other supported DMR networks. Configuration can involve your callsign, DMR ID, local hotspot frequency, colour code, timeslot/network settings and network credentials where required. Use a legal, appropriate frequency and the current instructions for your country and network.

D-STAR, YSF and M17

Compatible hotspot hardware and software may support several modes, including D-STAR, Yaesu System Fusion/YSF and M17. Support depends on the modem, firmware, software and network. Cross-mode features may also exist, but they do not mean every network or mode is automatically interconnected.

Hotspot versus repeater

Usually intended for short-range personal access.Usually designed to cover a wider local or regional area.

Your digital handheld → RF → personal hotspot → Internet → digital voice network → other users/repeaters/hotspots

Do I need a hotspot?

A hotspot can be useful when you cannot reach a suitable digital repeater, want personal access to a digital network, or want to experiment with supported modes. If a good local repeater already provides the network access you need, a hotspot may not be necessary.

Important setup tips

  • Use your own valid callsign and required network ID.
  • Choose an appropriate legal hotspot frequency for your location.
  • Keep firmware and hotspot software maintained.
  • Use strong unique passwords for dashboards and network services.
  • Do not expose an administration dashboard directly to the public internet without appropriate protection.
  • Check network rules before enabling bridges or cross-mode functions.
